# SUMMA questions and answers (BUDGpedia FAQ, 3 September 2026)

## Timeline of SUMMA releases

### When will SUMMA go live in my entity?

Following the rollout of SUMMA to the pilot agencies CINEA, ERA and EUROJUST (2022) and CAJU (2023), SUMMA is live for the Commission and Executive Agencies as of November 2024 for the anticipated budget and as of January 2025 for full budget operations

Other EU agencies and entities currently still using ABAC will be onboarded to SUMMA in two waves in January 2026 and January 2027. See the full list of planned SUMMA rollouts for these entities.

For the management of Business Partners, SUMMA is already live, including for those entities still using ABAC, and replaces ABAC's LEF/BAF module for the entry and maintenance of third parties and their bank accounts. The Business Partners created and updated in SUMMA are automatically replicated in ABAC, so there will be no impact on other ABAC processes.

### When is the next SUMMA release planned?

SUMMA will continue to evolve with improvements and new functionalities released on a quarterly basis. Exact dates will be announced in due course, see the calendar for details. For more information, please visit the SUMMA releases page.

## Change management

### What is the SUMMA Change Network?

During the pre-SUMMA go-live phase, the Change Network - consisting of Change Coordinators, Change Leads, and STIGs (SUMMA Transition and Implementation Groups) - ensured that key messages reached all end users within a DG/Service. Acting as 'the eyes and ears' at the local level, these change actors collectively gathered feedback in order to adjust the change approach accordingly. They then escalated this feedback to the SUMMA Programme Team via the defined channels and regular Change Network touchpoints.

Following the SUMMA go-live, the focus gradually shifts towards the Local SUMMA Coordinators, who become increasingly vital as the roles of the change actors fade. They take the lead in supporting and sustaining change, ensuring that all SUMMA users receive smooth, continuous operational support in their day-to-day activities. Moreover, they serve as the first point of contact for users, replacing the Change Coordinators in this regard, and will remain active after hypercare in view of the system's future evolutions and needs.

Please refer to the list of Change Leads and Change Coordinators and the list of Local SUMMA Coordinators appointed in each DG/Service, to identify the relevant contact points in your area.

### What were the responsibilities of the Change Coordinators?

The Change Coordinators were the single point of contact with the SUMMA Programme Team, overseeing and facilitating change activities within their DG/Service. Their responsibilities included:

Monitoring change readiness alongside the SUMMA Transition and Implementation Groups (STIGs) at local DG/Service level, and aligning with Change Lead; Reporting and discussing change readiness with DG BUDG; Participating in regular touchpoints with DG BUDG; Managing the STIGs to drive pre-go-live activities; Following up on implementation local change plans, such as internal communication, and other preparation, such as UATs; Identifying and addressing roadblocks and concerns with the Change Leads and/or the SUMMA Programme team.

### How did the SUMMA Programme team support the Change Network?

The SUMMA Programme team supported the Change Network by providing relevant materials and fulfilling key responsibilities related to change management including:

Facilitate recurring touchpoints with the Change Coordinators; Promote good change management practices and facilitate the exchange of experiences; Track, report and regularly communicate the progress and development of overall change readiness and progression towards the specific activities outlined in the Change Readiness Checklist; Ensure coordination with DG BUDG for training, UAT, technology, communication and organisational readiness activities; Provide and promote selected learning and communication materials (e.g., webinars and training announcements), including materials based on Change Network feedback; and Provide templates (e.g., communications, the Readiness Checklist and progress monitoring); Provide guidance and keep up to date high-level plan of change management activities during the transition to SUMMA; Provide any other support needed for specific issues.

### What is the Change Readiness Checklist?

The Change Readiness Checklist is a tool that was used to help each DG/Service monitor, track and follow up on preparatory activities that needed to be completed within the DG/Service in order to support the SUMMA rollout. In particular, the checklist helped DGs to identify actions to be implemented in five different activity areas: Testing, training, communication, technology and organisational preparedness. The tasks and activities on the checklist provided the basis for regularly tracking completion, monitoring change readiness feedback, and identifying next steps.

## User acceptance testing

### How important is the testing for the successful implementation of the programme?

Testing is essential for the successful implementation of SUMMA. It is the most effective means of ensuring that the system meets the requirements and is fit for purpose. During UAT, end users conduct tests that simulate real-world scenarios to ensure that the system meets their needs and functions as intended. This process ensures that any bugs or issues are identified and can be fixed before the system goes live.

Ahead of the SUMMA go-live on 1 January 2025, we have already held UAT sessions with the Commission and Executive Agencies. Our experience shows that involving committed, experienced key users with a constructive mindset is vital to success. The UAT sessions for the next wave of entities to be onboarded to SUMMA in January 2026 concluded on 11 July 2025. Additionally, UAT sessions for entities going live in January 2027 will take place next year. Exact dates will be communicated in due course.

### Is training foreseen before the User Acceptance Testing?

Before the UAT campaign begins, kick-off sessions are organised to explain the main differences between SUMMA and ABAC, cover the testing process and to help users familiarise themselves with the new navigation system. This short onboarding exercise makes it easier for users to get started with SUMMA. However, it is not intended as a substitute for regular end-user training, which enables users to become fully autonomous in using the new system.

Introductory training on specific topics, as well as hands-off workshops and info sessions, is available prior to the UAT. Key users are also encouraged to use the online training resources on BUDGpedia, including the SUMMA glossary, to better prepare for the UATs.

### How are the User Acceptance Testing sessions organised?

The UAT sessions organised by the SUMMA team are usually guided. After a brief introduction to the process to be tested and the test scenario(s) on the agenda, the testers execute the scenario(s) and document the results using a testing tool called Focused Build. The test scenarios provide very detailed instructions on what needs to be done. SUMMA experts are present to help participants execute the scenarios, providing step-by-step guidance and answering questions as needed.

### How much time should key users plan for the testing activities?

This depends on various factors, such as the number and duration of guided UAT sessions, involvement in additional exploratory testing after sessions, and the user's role during and after the session(s). Therefore, the estimate needs to be reassessed as the SUMMA programme progresses.

### What kind of feedback can testers provide to DG BUDG following the User Acceptance Testing?

Key users are asked to register test results and identified defects in a testing tool called Focused Build in a timely manner. This allows the SUMMA project team to analyse the overall results and track the resolution of defects. After each session, key users are given extra free testing time. This enables them to work through the scenario at their own pace, exploring variations and alternative scenarios to ensure the system meets their specific requirements.

### Could DG BUDG tailor the UAT sessions to the needs of each entity and increase the number of testers who participate?

We would have loved to tailor your sessions more. However, due to limited resources, we have to take a standardised approach to testing. Our priority is to ensure that the standard test scenarios work well for all entities that may require the tested business processes. These standard tests form the basis of SUMMA and are something that everyone should be familiar with.

Moreover, we encourage key users to perform additional free tests based on their understanding of the standard scenarios. This results in variations of the standard scenarios being tested that reflect real-life situations, addressing more specific cases relevant to their entities, and combining the building blocks of the sessions completed so far.

Furthermore, entities are encouraged to participate in or organise integration-focused UAT sessions. System owners typically play a leading role in such UATs.

### How does DG BUDG ensure that all crucial business scenarios are tested?

As SUMMA is already in production, extensive testing has been carried out on the system developments, and they have been approved for use. Entities that will be onboarded to SUMMA in 2026 and 2027 (with the exception of the EEAS, which has a specific development project) will receive a copy of the standard SUMMA template. Business Process Owners, with the help of Business Managers and experts, identify the key business processes that should be tested by end users in UAT sessions, to verify that the template is suitable and accurate for specific entities.

The UAT planning details, including the sessions and tested scenarios, are shared with the Task Force managing the entities’ transition, and the scenarios are tested during the UAT campaign.

### How do testers from onboarding entities gain access to the testing environment?

Access is provided by the entities themselves. During the onboarding process, each entity nominates an ExAM (External Authorisation Manager) and an ExAM backup. After receiving the relevant training, they provide the necessary permissions to access the SUMMA test environment for users who have been officially nominated by their entity via ARES or an equivalent system. Access to the test environment is granted on a need-to-have basis and is not open to anyone working at the entity.

### Is it possible to conduct free testing for entities that are onboarded in 2026 and 2027?

Yes, once the UAT campaign has finished and any critical issues have been reported, users of the onboarded entities will be granted access to the test environment to perform free testing. Access may be provided by the entity's ExAMs (External Authorisation Managers). DG BUDG does not provide access for UAT or free testing for onboarded entities. Users who previously participated in the UAT will retain their access and can perform free testing without any additional access procedures.

## Business partners

### Is the Single Electronic Data Interchange Area (SEDIA) used for registering every business partner, or only for procedures for which eSubmission is required?

SEDIA cannot be used for every single Business Partner. For example, 'staff' BPs are usually managed by the PMO. Currently, the general obligation is to use REA Validation Services for all eSubmission procedures. Please note that additional types of procedures will gradually use eSubmission in procurement, so creating and validating the PIC will be very valuable for future implementation.

### Are Business Partners created by one DG also available for the transactions of other DGs?

Yes, a BP created under one company code can be used by others. However, if the BP was created under a different company code - for example, a joint undertaking - you will first need to extend it to your company code in the SUMMA MDG. There is a specific button in the MDG ('Extend BP') that allows you to easily extend it to your own company code. Please note that it is not possible to extend a BP if it is blocked (the user will first have to request unblocking).

### When using SEDIA as an entry point, are the notions of Business Partner and PIC unified to avoid double validation of the PIC for existing Business Partners?

No, because the BP and the PIC (used in SEDIA/PDM) are different concepts that do not always correspond one-to-one. In accordance with the Financial Regulation, BPs registered in SUMMA are legal entities with legal personality (with the exception of ministries), or are treated as such. This is not always the case for PICs registered in PDM.

### In the event of mergers or takeovers, are the relevant changes replicated in the Purchase Orders, or do they need to be amended manually, as it was in ABAC?

This continues to work as it did in ABAC. In SUMMA, changes resulting from mergers or takeovers are not replicated automatically in Purchase Orders, requiring manual intervention by the user instead.

### Are there any controls to ensure authenticity of data provided by a BP, e.g. in the event of a change of bank account?

As per the Financial Regulation, it is the responsibility of the Initiating Agent (IA) to confirm the payer's identity. Like ABAC, SUMMA provides controls (business rules and algorithms) to confirm the validity of the data, but it is the IA who must confirm that it corresponds to the provided name. BUDG C3 also performs post-quality control checks on a sample of data to ensure the authenticity of the data and smooth payment to entities.

### What is a BP contact?

A BP contact is a way to register the contact details of a business partner (e.g. the name, address and phone number of a person or department).

### How should a company with different branches in other countries be registered?

As branches do not have legal personality, they cannot be registered as BPs. Only the company itself will be registered as a BP, while the branches’ bank accounts are under the mother company. The applicable rules remain the same in this respect and do not change with SUMMA.

### How are consortia/group members registered in SUMMA?

We only register a BP for a consortium if it has legal personality, just like we did in ABAC. Otherwise, the Purchase Order should be made out to one of the consortium members. Please click here for more information on consortia.

### Do BP checks include beneficial ownership?

No, as these are aimed at the completeness, relevance and accuracy of the data provided by the initiating services, this is not foreseen for post-validation checks.

### Is there mandatory training on BPs? If so, who is the target audience for this training?

Yes, mandatory training is provided for Initiating Agents who will be registering Business Partners in SUMMA. More information about training can be found here.

### Have the LEF/BAF that were already in ABAC been transferred to SUMMA? Or must DGs enter all historical BPs in SUMMA?

All the LEF/BAF created or used in the last 2 years, or having open transactions, were migrated to SUMMA before the go-live. If you are still using ABAC and wish to have a blocked LEF/BAF in SUMMA please contact BUDG-SUMMA-BP@ec.europa.eu. If you are working fully in SUMMA and need a BP that was not migrated, ABAC should not be regarded, and you should create a BP from scratch with all the necessary documentation (even if this entity existed before in ABAC). More information can be found here.

## Data migration

### What approach is used for data migration?

For the migration of the European Commission data and the subsequent data migration in 2026 and 2027, a greenfield approach will be applied. This involves migrating only open ABAC transactions while all closed transactions remain in ABAC, open for consultation. For full contract cycle monitoring, a dedicated data warehouse solution allows users to view closed ABAC transactions, as well as open SUMMA transactions in a single contract view in the 'Single view of file' report.

### What transaction data was migrated to SUMMA (Data Migration scope)?

Data migration included the following transactions:

All open transactions related to the legal commitment identified in ABAC at the end of the year (after the execution of the carry forward/carry over); All open transactions that are not related to a legal commitment, but relevant for business continuity.

More specifically, all of the following transactions were migrated:

Carried-forward/Carried-over budget appropriations; Open commitments ('RAL' - reste à liquider > 0) Budgetary commitments with open amount; Validated legal commitments with open budgetary commitments; Commitments with RAL = 0, but with open pre-financing still to be cleared in SUMMA; Open invoices (cost claim, commercial invoice, internal invoice, internal document); Open pre-financing payment (balance); Open forecasts of revenue with open amount; Open recovery orders with open amount; General Ledger 'GL' balance and open items; Budgetary Guarantees (LIGA); Inventoried items and balances.

### What transaction data was not migrated to SUMMA (Data Migration scope)?

Data migration did not include the following transactions:

Closed transactions.

More specifically, all the following transactions are not migrated:

Closed commitments ('RAL'- reste à liquider = 0) except if these have an open pre-financing still to be cleared; Closed budgetary commitments without open amount. Note that commitments level 1 with no open amount (RAC 'reste à contracter' = 0) still have to be migrated if at least one of their linked commitments level 2 has RAL >0. This is in line with the carry-forward process; Closed invoices: invoices fully cleared by a payment and/or by a pre-financing; Closed recovery orders: recovery without open amount; Closed forecast of revenues: forecast without open amount; Payments: payment requests and payment orders are not migrated; Framework contracts and procedures (managed by eProcurement).

### What master data was migrated to SUMMA (Data Migration scope)?

Data migration included the following master data:

Extension of legal entities: vendors, customers, bank accounts; Inventory: products (material), functional location, inventory balance/inventoried items (equipment in storage location/functional location); Fixed Assets.

### What is to be considered as an open transaction and a closed transaction in ABAC (Data Migration scope)?

DG BUDG released the Open transaction dashboard which simulates the scope of transactions selected for migration to SUMMA.

The definition of open and closed transactions is the following:

ABAC open transactions (migrated to SUMMA) are transactions either in the workflow or validated but with open amounts in ABAC ABAC closed transactions (not migrated to SUMMA) were completed in the past, and there are no further changes allowed on them in ABAC.

For more details, see the answers to questions 2 and 3.

### Will we get access to the SUMMA data warehouse with the migrated data after go-live?

Yes, the SUMMA data warehouse access remains available. Please consult the following pages dedicated to SUMMA reporting:

SUMMA reporting - access and procedures SUMMA dashboards.

### Will it be possible to do some sort of merger between data from the "frozen" ABAC DATA-WAREHOUSE and the dashboards?

All closed financial transactions in ABAC will remain available in the ABAC DWH. SUMMA datawarehouse will contain only a reduced set of ABAC data linked to open/ongoing transactions. Both ABAC and SUMMA data are already presented in the SUMMA dashboard called “Single View of File”, which allows contract financial monitoring with data coming from both ABAC and SUMMA.

### Will access to the ABAC warehouse remain open?

ABAC warehouse remains accessible. While you can still consult its content, editing is no longer possible.

### What will happen to the documents we have in ABAC? Will they be migrated?

Supporting documents are in scope and will be migrated to SUMMA.

## Workflows

### How many workflow phases are foreseen in SUMMA?

The number of the workflow phases in SUMMA depends on the type of transaction being performed. For certain key expenditure transactions requiring in-depth controls and checks, such as the creation of a Fund Reservation, a Purchase Order and a Payment, a classical workflow with 3 phases has been developed in SUMMA, while for the payment a 4th phase is added including the Accounting Officer validation.

This principle of having 3 or 4 phases must be applied to all transactions of the same type, irrespective of the level of difficulty of the controls performed. For the above-mentioned transactions, the classical workflow implemented in SUMMA includes the following 3 phases, in line with the Financial Regulation:

Initiation Verification Authorisation

However, for certain budgetary transactions, such as budget transfer validation, it has been decided to apply and develop a classical workflow involving only 2 phases (Initiating Agent / Authorising Officer). There's also a particularity for the invoice workflow with mandatories DEA (Data Entry Agent) and IA visas. With in addition an AO visa if there's a clearing related to the invoice.

A distinction must also be made between a 'phase' which implies a VISA from the User in the system and a 'step'. There are certain steps (actions) that can be performed by a user directly in SUMMA without giving a VISA. This is the reason why, for example, the goods receipt is posted in 1 step.

### Will there be a simple Initiating Agent workflow for creating Purchase Orders in SUMMA?

No, the creation of Purchase Order will be subject to a 3-phase workflow (Initiating Agent/Verifying Agent/Authorising Officer).

### Will there be a workflow tool for transactions not carried out in eGrants or eProcurement?

In its EC Release version, SUMMA foresees that all Procurement and Grant contracts will be managed in corporate solutions, such as e-Grants or e-Procurement. The standard workflow will apply for transactions to be processed directly in SUMMA.

### Can an Authorising Officer by delegation (AOD) decide to use a simplified procedure?

A predefined dedicated workflow is set up for each type of transaction in SUMMA. This is different from ABAC, where an Initiating Agent can choose its own workflow à la carte per type of transaction. The possibility of simplified workflows is being analysed.

### What will be the workflow of a cost claim in SUMMA, including reception, admissibility, eligibility, and how will the "stop the clock" of the different steps be implemented?

Invoices in ABAC have 2 steps:

Step 1: registration of the invoice in the accounts followed by Data Entry Agent visa. Step 2: analysis and verification of the invoice eligible amount and opening the possibility of a payment, followed by Initiating Agent visa.

The workflows for the invoices in SUMMA will have similar steps:

Invoice parked and saved: registration of the invoice in the accounts. Status set by DEA. The DEA also provide his visa. Invoice posted: analysis and verification of the invoice eligible amount and opening the possibility of a payment. Note that the budgetary information such as budgetary commitment, budget line, etc. is mandatory in SUMMA in order to post an invoice. IA performed this and provided his visa. If there is a clearing to be performed with the invoice there's a third visa to be provided by the AO.

SUMMA invoice posted = ABAC invoice + ABAC payment request.

The "stop the clock" is managed via the solution identified for suspensions and is similar to the functionalities existing in ABAC.

### Is there any integration between ARES validation workflows and SUMMA workflows?

No. This is not planned.

### Does the Initiating Agent's signature (process/terminate) have a financial impact (e.g. triggers budget consumption or accounting entry) or his/her involvement is merely procedural?

The simple creation of a Purchase Order triggers the blocking, reservation of the budget, whereas only a visa from the Authorising Officer Responsible (AOR) allows it to be consumed.

### Regarding security, ABAC required a password for validation, whereas in SUMMA it is just a click. Is additional security planned?

SUMMA relies on the secure corporate standard EU-Login for authentication. No additional re- authentication is required for validation.

### Does SUMMA allow extra light workflows for the invoice and payment directive, which consists of only 2 visa steps?

No, this is not foreseen. The pre-financing workflow was subject of a RUF working group in May- June 2022. The results were presented at the RUF in July 2022. It was decided that the workflow for pre-financing would also consist of three distinct and separate phases (Initiation, Verification and Authorisation), in line with the Financial Regulation.

The shorter workflow (IA-AOR) was not adopted for two reasons:

SAP/SUMMA only allows one workflow model per type of transaction and the working group was asked to find the most appropriate one. There are several departments in the Commission that have requested to have a separate verification visa as there are pre-financing payments that are more complex and deserve accurate and thorough verification before the authorisation.

While in some ABAC workflow models the verification is implicitly embedded in the AO visa, in SUMMA there needs to be an explicit visa to ensure that the verification has been carried out prior to the authorisation. Initiation and verification are mandatory phases performed by two different actors in compliance with the four-eyes principle of the Financial Regulation.

In this framework, with the SUMMA 3-step workflow, it is possible to allocate the FVA and AOS tasks to the same user and, despite the need to click twice in the system, the actual execution time can be faster than a single visa in ABAC. This was demonstrated during the UAT organised in May – June 2023.

The same principles explained above apply to de-commitments.

## Expenditure

### What does the rationalisation of the chart of accounts consist of?

The rationalisation of the Chart of Accounts is aimed at giving more structure to the numbering of the General Ledger Accounts (GLA), so that we can easily distinguish between assets and liabilities, current and non-current etc. The impact on typical users will be limited, as several mechanisms have been put in place to ensure the automatic derivation of the GLAs. In the P2P flows, SUMMA automatically derives the GLA code based on the information registered in the legal commitment (e.g. Purchase Order, the type of contract and the type of items ordered).

### Will the Purchase Order remain active/open if we pay out 100% of the amount as Pre-Financing?

A Purchase Order is not only the equivalent of a RAL (Reste à Liquider) in a level 2 commitment, but also the registration of the legal commitment, as well as the breakdown and the list of "materials", services purchased, or actions as part of a grant. As long as there are costs or expenses to be accepted, the Purchase Order is in fact available for the goods receipt (GR) or service entry sheets (SES) to allow the acceptance of costs and the clearing of pre-financing.

### What is the procedure for clearing pre-financing in SUMMA?

The clearing of Pre-Financing is completely covered by SUMMA. When processing invoices, the system informs about the pre-financing paid (down payment) in order to warn the financial agents about the possibility of encoding the corresponding clearing in a special tab of the invoice.

### Does a Suspension in SUMMA suspend both Commitment and Payment?

The solution for suspending invoices and payments (pre-financing or normal payments) is similar to ABAC. This solution meets the requirements of the Financial Regulation or legal commitments. Suspensions in SUMMA can be "total" or "partial". If an invoice is partially suspended, a partial payment is still possible. No suspension of the Purchase Order (PO) is foreseen at this stage.

### Use of Service Entry Sheets/Goods Receipt: Who are the candidates to use Goods Receipt (GR)?

All DGs (except OIB and OIL) and Ex. Agencies should use exclusively Goods Receipt for purchased goods and services (as explained on page 48 of this SUMMA manual) and for grants (as explained on page 77 of this SUMMA manual ).

### Use of Service Entry Sheets/Goods Receipt: Who are the candidates to use Service Entry Sheets (SES)?

DGs and Ex. Agencies that have not previously utilised the Service Entry Sheet (SES) in ABAC should refrain from using it in SUMMA. Currently, SES is exclusively available for use by the OIB and OIL.

### Use of Service Entry Sheets/Goods Receipt: What steps should I take to begin using SES?

Please note that the use of Lean Service Entry Sheets is included in the E-Procurement system roadmap and will be made available to all DGs in a future release. Following the release of the E- Procurement corporate solution into production, SUMMA's Lean Service Entry Sheets will be made available to all DGs and Executive Agencies. If you are interested in exploring the future use of SES and aligning this process with the specific requirements of your DG, please contact the SUMMA Expenditure Team. You can arrange a meeting by contacting the EC SUMMA Helpdesk.

### Use of Service Entry Sheets/Goods Receipt: Is there any difference from an accounting perspective between using service entry sheets (SES) or the goods receipt (GR) process?

No, there is no difference. Regardless of whether DG choose materials classified under 3* or 4*, both processes will reference the same GL account, as the assigned material group remains consistent.

### Use of Service Entry Sheets/Goods Receipt: Can I change the material from 3* to 4* on migrated POs?

No, materials should not be changed on migrated POs, as this may lead to unforeseen issues within the purchase process flow. Additionally, such changes will not affect GL accounting. The SUMMA Expenditure process flow for all DGs (except OIB and OIL) use the Goods Receipt (GR) process by default, following:

Goods Receipt – linked to 3* material codes. Standard Service Entry Sheets – no material is required (this specific solution design applies only to OIB & OIL). Lean Service Entry Sheets (not permitted for use in 2025, to be supported by E-Procurement in the future) – linked to 4* material codes. In case you have doubts please reach EC SUMMA Helpdesk.

### Changes in GL Accounts

There has been a dedicated SUMMA Clinic about GL accounts. Please consult the recording, presentation and infographics for further information.

### Changes in GL Accounts: How is the GL account determined in purchase orders?

The GL account is determined based on the material group assigned to the selected PO material. This means that users are unable to modify the GL account manually. The correct selection of the material is crucial to ensuring the appropriate GL account is applied.

### Changes in GL Accounts: Can I change the GL account in a PO item without changing the material?

No, the correct selection of the material is essential to ensure the appropriate assigned GL account.

### Changes in GL Accounts: What should I do if I notice an incorrect GL account in a migrated PO?

It is advisable to avoid making changes to migrated documents unless necessary. Instead, you should assess whether the discrepancy impacts accounting:

Verify the amount and determine if it affects the EC level reporting. Review the category of the GL account. In most cases, both the incorrect and correct GL accounts belong to the same category (e.g., commercial services, distinct from core business activities such as subsidies). As a result, such errors typically do not affect the interpretation of the EC financial statements.

If, after this reflection, you still believe that GL account needs to be corrected on migrated POs, please refer to the guidance on "How should I post-process a migrated PO if I notice it needs correction?" for further instructions.

### Changes in GL Accounts: How should I post-process a migrated PO if I notice it needs a GL account correction and/or WBS code (*in case of PO related to IGIA)?

The corrective action depends on the status of the purchase order (PO): Please note that the following corrective actions are not applicable to pre-financing lines of the purchase order. Scenario 1: POs where the invoice has already been validated and paid Invoice or Goods Receipt (GR) correction is not possible. For material amounts, the accounting team can make a correction entry at year-end to reclassify the amounts to the correct WBS code(*) and/or expense account, but this is only applicable if the amounts are material. Information regarding the materiality threshold is provided annually through the closure instructions. Scenario 2: For POs that are still open with future payments pending An amendment to the migrated PO is needed to affect the future GR and Invoice. To do this, de-commit the migrated PO item for the remaining open amount and create a new line item. If the PO item is not linked to a fund reservation, please contact helpdesk support to restore the remaining budget line. Be sure to provide a complete list of the affected POs. The amendment will initiate a new workflow process. Scenario 3: For POs where the invoice has been validated but not yet paid, and is linked to fund reservation For such cases, please proceed with the following steps: 1. Cancel the invoice and the GR document. 2. Mark the migrated item as deleted. 3. Duplicate the migrated Purchase Order (PO) item to create a new entry and update the material code and/or WBS Code (*) accordingly. Ensure that the quantity and amount remain unchanged to avoid any impact on the budget. This amendment will trigger the initiation of a new workflow process.

Scenario 4: For POs where the invoice has been validated but not yet paid, not linked to fund reservation In general, if a PO originating from previous years (migrated) and not linked to funds reservation, is de-committed, then a potential removal of corresponding commitment appropriations would occur, e.g. if PO was on funds which cannot be legally reused like VOBU/2024. In that situation, please proceed with the following steps: 1. Cancel both the invoice and the GR document. 2. Mark the migrated item as deleted. 3. Contact the helpdesk to restore the budget and provide the full list of affected PO items. This request will be analysed, and available budget will be restored if justified. 4. Duplicate the migrated PO item to create a new item and adjust the material code and/or WBS Code (*). No changes should be made to the quantity or amount to avoid impacts on budget. This amendment will trigger the initiation of a new workflow process.

Scenario 5: For POs with no invoice or GR Please update the material in the PO item. Ensure that there are no changes to the quantity or amount in order to avoid any impact on the budget. Scenario 6: For POs with parked invoices and no GR Please proceed with the following steps: Delete the parked invoice linked to the PO item. Update the material and/or WBS Code (*) in the PO item and ensure that there are no changes to the quantity or amount to avoid any impact on the budget .

Regarding the PO related to IGIA projects for which a WBS Code is used, taking into account the number of transactions concerned and that correcting the GL account and WBS CODE on the migrated POs is difficult (see information above), you are not expected to correct in all migrated POs the incoherences in GL accounts and WBS Code, you can proceed with mass correction using G* postings in SUMMA for year-end corrections.

### Changes in GL Accounts: What should I do if I need to issue my invoice to a different GL account for the same contract?

For newly created contracts, select the material code at the PO item level that best represents the predominant nature of the service, especially if you anticipate invoicing against different GL accounts. For migrated POs, please refer to the guidance on "How should I post-process a migrated PO if I notice it needs correction?" for further instructions.

### Post-processing of migrated procurement contracts from ABAC Assets/ABAC Workflow: How should I proceed to handle the migrated services and supply contracts initially managed in ABAC Assets?

The procurement contracts initially managed in ABAC Assets mu st be amended and processed within ABAC Assets. Data related to amendments, good receipt, invoices and equipment will flow to SUMMA through the integration between the two systems. If you encounter any issues or require further clarification regarding the post-processing of migrated procurement contracts, the e-Procurement Helpdesk team is available to assist you. For more information, please refer to the relevant user guide, which details the two approaches used for the migration of procurement contracts from ABAC Assets and ABAC Workflow to SUMMA.

### I am currently unable to find the migrated commitments for internal legal entities (LE Key 6999*). What I should I do?

Intra-Commission DGs transactions are excluded from the migration scope, as they follow a distinct creation process that is fully compatible with the SUMMA intra-billing solution. These transactions will be generated in bulk by the SUMMA solution team and communicated to the users accordingly.

## Budget structure

### Can we budget only by unit? When defining the granularity of the budget, is it possible to define different granularity level for operational budget lines and for administrative budget lines?

You will be able to manage the budget at unit, directorate or DG level and post on the same or different levels.

DGs have a possibility to choose between:

Budgeting and posting at the same level: DG, Directorate, Unit; or Budgeting at higher level (DG) and posting at lower level (Directorate, Unit or delegation like INTPA for example). Different granularity for administrative/operational budget or shared/direct management budget. The DGs' preferences have been communicated to DG BUDG. The conclusions have been summarised in the SUMMA Working Group on Budget Structure.

### What about administrative and prerogative budget lines in the funded programme?

The funded programme works similarly to an ABAC local position. It represents a local extension in the budget line and is part of the budget structure. Funded programmes that have been defined for SUMMA together with DGs/Executive Agencies are in the majority of cases derived from ABAC local positions but transformed for SUMMA following data rationalisation and better master data governance objectives. If a DG would like to break down their budget beyond the already available elements of the budget structure element (e.g. Funds Centre, Commitment Item), the Funded Programme can be used to have budget control at a lower level. If such a lower granularity is only needed for reporting purposes, only the WBS shall be used with reference to the Funded Programme “EMPTY”.

### In the case of granularity at unit level and expenditure directly linked to the Director-General or Deputy Director-General, what operational unit should be indicated?

There is no possibility to introduce the organisational data (responsible organisation in ABAC) at transaction level. The choice of the granularity of the fund centre is crucial for your later reporting. Therefore, there will be no reporting data at unit level when the fund centre is at DG or directorate level.

### How will the fund management work for co-delegated budget be handled – by the Operational Unit of the receiving DG or by the co-delegating DG?

The operational unit will be in charge of managing the file (receiving DG).

### What is the "Unit" that will appear in SUMMA budget structure? Is it the one providing the "certified correct" or the Authorising Officer by Sub-Delegation unit?

The unit is defined as the operational unit, the main responsible for the project or file.

### What is meant by posting?

In SUMMA, there will be budget addresses (where the budget is inscribed) and posting addresses (where the budget is consumed). This concept also exists today in ABAC (Credit versus Consumption lines).

### Will a line that is used by 10 units result in 10 lines?

Yes, one budget address for each unit.

### In case of reorganisations, files do not always follow the new units. Will the reorganisation updates in SYSPER be applied automatically?

In such cases, DG BUDGET would need to be informed to perform a custom re-assignment.

In case of partial reorganisations, the DG will need to indicate the part of the files that need to be reassigned.

### Is the budget availability checked at WBS level?

The budget availability is checked at the Funded Program/Budget Address level.

### Can "project keys" be interpreted as calls within a work programme?

Project keys are individual references of a project. The funded program is the overarching level for a group of projects. The call ID could become the funded program.

### Would it be up to the eGrants system to create the budget addresses in SUMMA since the project ID is part of the address?

No. Budget addresses are created only by a specific user intervention (generate budget addresses elements).

### Could we also make transfers in N+1 if the same constraint remains as it is now for the CL1 in ABAC?

The proposed way forward is to encode estimated amounts by unit and modify these amounts once the exact allocations are known. DG BUDGET has issued a SUMMA business requirement to allow re-allocating amounts within a fund reservation or between funds reservations even from previous years.

### How will you manage H7 administrative budgets in the Funded Programme?

The definition of Funded Programmes is up to the DGs, who can define them according to their specific business requirements to add more detail to their budget line. In case further advice on Funded Programmes is needed, a request for further guidance can be sent to the SUMMA Helpdesk.

### What will be the procedure for the prerogative budget lines that are used by the entire DG?

Such commitments could be fully allocated to a central unit managing common expenditure.

### What about a provisional commitment used to cover expenditure for the whole DG (i.e., mainly administrative expenditure - missions of staff, salaries).

Such commitments could be fully allocated to a central unit managing common expenditure.

### Who will have the responsibility / duty in SUMMA to internally transfer the credits in a co-delegated type II entity (i.e., within the executive agencies' budget lines)?

Transfer responsibilities do not change compared to ABAC.

### Given that the budget address in ABAC is more "flexible" than in SUMMA due to local extension, do you envisage to already standardise the budget lines in ABAC to allow for transparent migration from ABAC to SUMMA?

No, this is not yet planned.

### Should funds be re-allocated if budget line is used by more units, when the flexibility clause in the financing decisions is used?

Yes, this is the impact.

### Could you provide more information on fund centres broken down by operational unit?

The breakdown of fund management centres per operational unit is being discussed in the ad- hoc working group on Budget Structure organised by the RUF.

### Which are the DGs that do not use the unit concept?

A BO report providing the use of the unit in the Com L2 Responsible organisation field is available in the ABAC DWH.

### Will forecasts take place by unit, too?

The bi-annual forecast is available at the level of unit, too. In practical terms, the DGs must forecast at the same granularity level they chose their budgeting and posting in SUMMA: DG, Directorate, and/or unit.

### How and where will the dimensions 'programming period' and 'fund' be encoded and reported?

DG BUDG has worked on an integrated MFF structure combining different views (legacy programs used by accounting, programs including contributions from other programs for performance reporting etc.). This alternative MFF view shows the fund and its MFF period in a dedicated field in the budget structure (functional area). This alternative view does not affect the number of budget lines as it can be derived from the budget line. The functional area is standard SAP field in the budget structure, available in other SAP modules as accounting.

### Is it necessary for harmonising local references through the encoding of WBS to have the funded programme as a budget structure?

The local reference stored into SUMMA must correspond to a genuine project number in the local system to allow linking local and SUMMA data and reducing the need of copying SUMMA data into local systems.

### How should we manage L2 commitment under L1, when L1 is managed by one unit and L2 by different units?

This problem already exists in the External Aid DGs and is managed through decommitments or manual interventions in ABAC. The proposed way forward is to encode estimated amounts by unit and modify these amounts once the exact allocations are known. DG BUDG has issued a SUMMA business requirement to allow re-allocating amounts within a fund reservation or between funds reservations even from previous years.

### Have you considered that when the budget structure grows, the number of transfers grows too?

This situation would only arise if the budget was managed at unit level. DG BUDG proposal is to give DGs flexibility to post at unit level while managing the budget at DG level. Therefore, the number of transfers will remain unaffected. For the DGs already budgeting at local line level, the number of transfers will remain equal compared to ABAC.

### Can we eliminate local lines at unit level with the new MFF?

Local lines are used in many areas of the budget. Programming at unit level was one of the main requests. Managing the budget at unit level will be optional in SUMMA. Only posting at unit level is foreseen, which is very close to the current way of working in shared management, where the unit is encoded at commitment level. Posting the commitment at unit level will eliminate the need of indicating the unit in all transactions.

### Will SUMMA include the environments/transactions related to the Trust Funds (i.e., ABAC T004, T005)?

Trust Funds will not be migrated to SUMMA, the open transactions should be fully paid by end of 2026 in ABAC.

### In the case of Global (CL1) commitments, usually, the details of the operational units are not known when those commitments are created. Which unit will need to be indicated and how will the split in the individual commitments be done?

The L1 details should contain the best guess for a share. The share can be adjusted even in n+1.

### Depending on the granularity level chosen, what will be the impact on the Budget Forecast I.T tool? And what will be the impact on the hearings details requested?

The functioning of BPC Forecasting tool remains the same, but its setup has been enhanced as to display all the fund centers available for a given DG. Each fund centre must be opened individually, and the belonging budget lines forecasted, saved, and published. This guarantees that all budget lines are forecasted, and any potential under-forecast avoided. There is no impact on the budget hearings process.

### In the case of granularity at unit level, can DG BUDG confirm that this level will improve the integration in the future MAPP IT Tool? What will be the impact if DIR level or DG level is chosen?

DG BUDG cannot assess the level of granularity used in MAPP.

### Regarding the budgetary commitment, are there any other places/possibilities where the name of the operational unit in charge of the commitment/payment can be identified?

The main goal is to have a centralised field where the organisational information is available. Being in the budget structure, all subsequent transactions inherit this information automatically without further encoding.

### Will the various cascade levels of the budget structure still be visible in SUMMA?

And will the division of responsibilities be done accordingly? Co-delegations are visible in the proposal.

### Will priority of fund sources become semi-automatic due to the limited number available (C1-C7)?

It will be possible to modify the funds source to apply priority rules. Automation was not foreseen for the go-live, as this is not a core functionality.

### How do we check the validity period of the appropriations (commitments and payments)?

The budget period master data contain start and end dates for the use of appropriations.

### When defining the fund centre, how will the structure be defined? Will it be according to COMREF? Is there a special treatment for the special units that are not allocated to a given Directorate and are directly dependant from the Director General or DDG?

The Fund Centre will synchronise with Sysper (=COMREF) data.

### How will the transfer of projects be managed in SUMMA?

SUMMA will reassign open commitment amounts to another budget address.

### How should we correct the budget structure in a Purchase Order or in a payment when realising that the budget line selected is incorrect?

A regularisation payment should be performed if the payment is already validated.

### Can transfer of funds between different fund centres be managed by the DG?

Transfers between units can be performed within the DG, in analogy to current transfers on local lines.

### Why will there be no possibility to differentiate between commitment and payment appropriations in SUMMA?

There is always a possibility to differentiate commitment and payment appropriations. Both appropriations are stored in different ledgers. For Non-Differentiated budget addresses, both appropriations are always equal. For Differentiated budget addresses, commitment and payment appropriations may differ.

### If the funded programme is included in the budget line structure, this will result in managing many budget lines per funded programmes. Do we consider funded programme the whole IPA III fund 2021-2027?

The dimension allows the DG decentralised to define more detail for the budget line. Each DG can freely choose whether to use Funded Programme or not. In case they do not want to add more detail, the default value EMPTY will be used for all their Budget Structure Elements. The advantage of defining values can be: To have budget control on a more detailed level Provide reporting information, e.g. by yearly work programme

### There is a new functionality of funds reservation (L1 commitment) in SUMMA allowing a modification of amounts allocated. What does the joint thematic call NEAR-INTPA require for processing L1 commitment in INTPA and then sharing the funds with NEAR? Does it cover only NDICI or IPA III as well?

The re-allocation facility on funds reservations will be available for the entire Commission and all programmes.

### Will the terms "Funded Programme" and "Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)" have the same or a similar relationship as ABAC "Level 1 Commitments" and "Level 2 Commitments" from an operational viewpoint?

In ABAC Terms, the equivalent to Funded Programme would rather be the local position, for WBS the posting criteria – however, both elements differ in many ways from the functionalities of the former ABAC fields. A WBS actually derives the funded programme in an expenditure document, the Funded Programme cannot be selected by its own. The Equivalent of a Level 1 Commitment would be the Funds Reservation, for a Level 2 Commitment the Purchase Order.

### Could you foresee the introduction of posting criteria related to specific crisis situations, such as in Ukraine and Syria, without going through a heavy technical/procedural update process, allowing the trace of funds to address these crisis situations?

In SUMMA, tracking country-specific expenditure is possible via the Tagging Mechanism (the ‘ASSOCNTRY’ tag). The same functionality is used for tracking expenditure related to Ukraine via the ‘BUD_UA_EXP’ tag. The Tagging Mechanism does not require a full workload and it is easy to set up in the SUMMA core system. However, it should only be used when standard SUMMA fields are not available for reporting purposes. More information about the mechanism is available on the SUMMA master data page, where instructions on the use of the ‘BUD_UA_EXP’ tag are provided. Instructions for using the ‘ASSONTRY’ tag will be published soon.

### In the case of joint thematic calls, could DG BUDG technically foresee a smooth change of the lead role of DGs specific cases in the future for which another DG might be asked to take the lead?

If a DG is to take the lead in a call, the Funds Reservation should be encoded by that DG. If this cannot be done, a re-allocation requires decommitting and re-committing the funds and transferring funds to another DG.

### Does budget inscription need Authorising Officer Sub-Delegated validation?

There is no Authorising Officer Sub-delegated validation currently for budget inscription and it will remain the same for SUMMA.

### Which workflow is to be used for credits transfers (CODEL II/III, internal adjustments)?

Two step workflow (Initiating Agent + Authorising Officer) for: adjustments between budget line extensions (in ABAC terms: ‘between central extensions of the budget position’) rebalancing adjustments between type1 co-delegations autonomous transfers adjustments and transfers managed by executive agencies and external entities No workflow (i.e., only 1 agent) for: adjustments within budget line extensions (in ABAC terms: ‘within central extensions of the budget position’) adjustments within the same post, to/between sub-items of the Office’s budget line adjustments from/to types 2 and 3 co-delegations, Fund Center at Directorate or unit level, Funded Program chargeback adjustments

### Do you foresee a specific user group to address budgetary issues, such as CODEL? And if yes, when should it be expected?

No specific user group is foreseen for co-delegations. If it should be created, please share with us the topics to be discussed via BUDG-SUMMA@ec.europa.eu

### What is the format of Account Assignment?

Account Assignment is a set of 6 independent dimensions, which constitute a budgeting address (can hold appropriations) or posting address (can hold consumption): Funds Source, Budget Period, Funds Management Center, Commitment Item, Functional Area, Funded Program. Each of the 6 dimensions is encoded seperately in SUMMA, e.g. when posting transcations or querying information on available appropriations. For reporting purposes, it may be represented as a single ID composed of these fields in that specific order and separated by:

'/' if there is no such character in any of the attributes '|' if there is the character '/' in some of the attributes

Examples of valid text representations of Account Assignments:

VOBU/2022/INTPA.C.C2/E.14.020120/NDICI_21_27/INTPA

EARN/N|2022|INTPA|E.14029902.03|DCI_14_20|EMPTY

EARN/N|2022|INTPA|E.14029902.03|DCI_14_20|EMPTY
